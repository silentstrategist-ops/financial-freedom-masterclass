Diagnosis Before

A generic package sold before any real diagnosis gets compared on price. A proposal built directly from a real diagnosis gets judged on relevance.

The Real Value of Diagnosis

Clients are experts in their business, but rarely experts in their blind spots. "My team isn't performing" is a symptom. The real cause could be a broken process, a hiring mistake, or a complete lack of decision-making authority.

The Four Pillars of Diagnostic Questions

Instead of pitching, ask questions in these four categories.

1. Concrete Reality
"Walk me through exactly what happened the last time a deadline was missed."
2. Previous Attempts
"What have you already tried to fix this, and why do you think it failed?"
3. Untested Assumptions
"Why do you believe lack of delegation is the problem rather than lack of a clear process?"
4. Concrete Outcomes
"If we fix this completely, what specific, measurable thing looks different in 90 days?"
Step 44 Integration

Plan Your Own Approach

Question 01Think of a problem you've helped solve before. What did the person originally think the problem was, and what did you find it actually was once you looked closely?
Question 02Write three real diagnostic questions you could genuinely ask a new client in your own field.
Question 03Have you ever jumped straight to offering a solution before fully understanding the real problem? What actually happened?
Question 04What assumption do you think most people in your industry carry that's never actually been properly tested?
Question 05If someone came to you with a problem in your area of expertise today, what would your genuine first question be?
Question 06Think of a time someone diagnosed a problem of yours correctly before offering a solution. How did that change how much you trusted them?
