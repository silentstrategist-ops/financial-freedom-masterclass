Why This Chapter Exists

The previous two chapters covered the two most durable, reliable engines of an advisory or coaching practice: coaching itself, and the relationships that quietly feed it. This chapter is different. It's a practical tour of the actual, current landscape of ways to get paid for judgment and experience, the specific platforms, roles, and models available right now, in the current market. Some of what's covered here will still be true in ten years. Some of it reflects the specific tools and platforms available today. Both are worth knowing, because the goal isn't just understanding the timeless principles, it's actually landing your first real, paid conversation as soon as possible.

Why "Get A Job" Is No Longer The Only Real Option

For most of the last century, a career in mid to late life had two broad tracks: stay employed somewhere, or fully retire. That binary has quietly broken apart. Between full-time employment and full retirement now sits an entire spectrum of ways to be paid for expertise without either extreme, fractional roles, advisory retainers, expert-call platforms, licensed frameworks, board seats, and more. None of these existed in their current form even fifteen years ago, and most of them are specifically well suited to someone with real, accumulated experience rather than someone just starting out.

This matters enormously for someone transitioning out of a long corporate or professional career. The market has quietly built an entire new set of doors specifically for people in your position, doors that didn't exist for the previous generation making this same transition.

Fractional Roles: A Real Executive Title, Without The Full-Time Commitment

One of the fastest-growing categories on this modern menu is the fractional executive role. A fractional Chief Operating Officer, fractional Chief Marketing Officer, or fractional Chief Financial Officer does real, senior-level executive work for a company, but on a part-time or defined-hours basis, often serving several companies at once rather than being tied to just one.

This model exists because a huge number of small and mid-sized companies genuinely need senior-level strategic thinking, someone who has actually run operations, marketing, or finance at scale before, but cannot justify, or simply cannot afford, a full-time executive salary and the benefits that come with it. A fractional arrangement solves this cleanly. The company gets real, experienced judgment at the top of its organization. The fractional executive gets meaningful, well-paid work without being locked into a single employer, and can often run two or three such engagements simultaneously.

This is a genuinely strong fit for someone who spent decades in a specific functional role, operations, finance, marketing, HR, supply chain, inside a larger company. The exact title and function you held is very often directly transferable into a fractional version of that same role, just spread across a handful of smaller companies instead of concentrated inside one large one.

AI-Adjacent Advisory: A Newer, Fast-Growing Category

A newer category, but one growing extremely quickly, is AI-adjacent advisory. This isn't necessarily about being a technical AI expert or a software engineer. It's about helping companies figure out, practically and sensibly, how new AI tools actually fit into their existing operations, where they genuinely save time and money, where they introduce real risk, and how to roll them out without disrupting a functioning team.

This category is unusually welcoming to experienced professionals rather than only rewarding the technically youngest people in the room, for a specific reason. Companies exploring how to use AI tools don't just need someone who understands the technology. They need someone who understands their actual business, their actual operations, their actual regulatory environment, and can translate a new tool into a sensible, safe, well-sequenced rollout. That combination, real operational judgment plus a working understanding of the new tools, is exactly what a mid-career professional willing to learn the tools can offer, and it's currently in very high demand relative to the supply of people who can genuinely do it well.

Why This Menu Favors Experience, Not Youth

It's worth pausing on something that runs through every option on this modern menu: almost none of it rewards raw youth over real experience. A fractional COO role needs someone who has actually run operations before. An AI-adjacent advisory engagement needs someone who understands a real business, not just a new tool. This is a meaningful shift from how the previous generation often thought about consulting and advisory work, as something reserved for young, freshly graduated analysts from prestigious firms. The modern menu, in large part, was built around the exact opposite: monetizing decades of lived, specific experience, applied flexibly rather than locked inside a single full-time job.

How To Think About Choosing From This Menu

The rest of this chapter walks through several specific categories on this menu in more detail: expert-call and freelance marketplaces, licensed frameworks and digital products, board-level advisory, and the AI tools that help you deliver all of it more efficiently. Not every category will fit every person, and that's fine. The right starting point depends heavily on what you actually did in your career, how much you already have that's easy to package (a proven process, a clear specialty, a strong professional reputation), and how much hands-on time you genuinely want to spend versus how much you'd rather build once and reuse.

A useful way to think about it: some of what's on this menu trades your time directly for money, fractional roles, expert calls, board seats. Some of it is built specifically to decouple your time from your income, licensed frameworks and digital products being the clearest example. Most successful mid-career advisory practices end up blending a few of these together rather than betting everything on just one.

Reflect And Apply

Answer these fully in your journal.

1. Which functional area did you spend the most real years working in, operations, finance, marketing, sales, HR, something else? Could you picture yourself doing a lighter, part-time version of that exact role for a smaller company?

2. How comfortable are you, honestly, with newer AI tools right now? Is this a genuine area of curiosity for you, or does it feel like a stretch?

3. Of the two broad approaches described above, trading time directly for money versus building something once to sell repeatedly, which appeals to you more right now, and why?

4. Think of a smaller company you're familiar with, maybe through your network, that likely couldn't afford a full-time senior executive but could genuinely use one part-time. What would that role look like for them?

5. What's one skill or piece of knowledge from your career that you assumed was "just part of the job" at the time, but that you now realize is genuinely valuable and hard to find in the market?
