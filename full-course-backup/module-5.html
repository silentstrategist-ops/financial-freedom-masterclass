One Number Was Never Going To Be Enough

You already calculated your wealth in months. That number matters, but it only measures one thing, how long your cash would last if income stopped completely. It doesn't tell you how likely that stop actually is, how exposed you are to smaller shocks that never fully stop your income but quietly bleed it, or how fast you could recover if something did go wrong.

Fragility isn't one number. It's a shape, made up of several different exposures stacked on top of each other. Two people can have identical savings and completely different levels of real risk, because one of them has four separate income streams and no debt, and the other has one employer, a large loan, and zero backup plan if that employer disappears tomorrow. Same wealth-in-months figure. Wildly different reality.

This section builds the fuller picture. Go through each part properly. This isn't a quiz to rush through, it's an honest audit of exactly where you're standing, so nothing you build afterward is built on a floor you haven't actually inspected.

Exposure One: Income Concentration

The single biggest hidden fragility most working adults carry isn't debt, it's dependence on one source of income.

If one employer, one client, or one contract represents all, or nearly all, of your monthly income, you don't just have a job. You have a single point of failure. It doesn't matter how stable that employer feels right now, stability is a feeling, not a guarantee, and every stable-feeling job eventually meets a restructuring, an acquisition, an economic downturn, or simply a decision made in a room you weren't in.

Work this out properly. What percentage of your total monthly income currently comes from a single source? Write the actual number, not an estimate rounded down to sound better than it is.

If that number is 100%, you are carrying maximum income concentration risk, regardless of how much you're earning or how much you've saved. A high salary from one source is not diversification. It's a large amount of exposure to a single point of failure.

Exposure Two: Debt Composition, Not Just Debt Amount

Two people can owe the exact same total amount and carry very different levels of real fragility, because what matters isn't only the number, it's the shape of the debt.

Ask three questions about every debt you're carrying. Is the interest rate high or low? Is the repayment fixed or does it move with market rates? Could the lender demand full repayment or seize an asset quickly if you missed payments, or is there flexibility built in?

A large, low-interest, flexible mortgage is a very different creature to a smaller but high-interest, rigid personal loan. Total debt gives you a headline number. Debt composition tells you how much pressure that debt can actually apply to you in a bad month, and how fast that pressure can escalate.

Write it down. List every debt you currently carry, the amount, the interest rate, and whether the repayment terms are flexible or rigid. Total fragility isn't just the sum at the bottom. It's how many of those debts are rigid and high-interest at the same time, because those are the ones that turn a temporary income dip into a genuine crisis.

Exposure Three: Re-Employability Speed

If your main income stopped tomorrow, how long would it realistically take you to replace it, even partially?

This is different from your wealth-in-months number, which measures how long your savings last. This measures how long the wound would actually stay open. Someone with three months of savings but skills that are in high demand and a strong professional network might realistically replace lost income within weeks. Someone with six months of savings but a narrow, highly specialised skill set tied to one shrinking industry, and no active network, might take the better part of a year to recover equivalent income. The second person, despite looking more stable on paper, is often more fragile in practice.

Answer honestly. If you lost your main income source this month, how many weeks or months do you estimate it would take you to replace at least half of it? What would you actually do first? Who would you actually call? If you're not sure, that uncertainty is itself useful information, it tells you that part of your fragility work isn't financial at all, it's making sure your skills and network stay active and visible even while you're comfortably employed.

Exposure Four: The Protection Gap

A liquidity buffer handles small shocks, a broken appliance, an unexpected bill, a slow month. It was never designed to handle the large ones, a serious illness, a disability, a death, an accident. Those events don't dent a buffer. They can erase years of progress in a single event, and they're exactly the kind of risk that feels unlikely enough to ignore, right up until it isn't.

We'll build this out properly in a later module, but for the diagnosis to be honest, it needs to be named here. Answer plainly. Do you currently have any form of protection, insurance or otherwise, against a serious medical event, disability, or the loss of the household's main earner? If the honest answer is no, that's not a failure, it's simply an accurate reading of a real gap, and it belongs in your fragility picture right now, not just in a future module.

Putting The Shape Together

You now have four separate readings, alongside the wealth-in-months number from the last module. Not to average into one score, fragility doesn't compress cleanly into a single digit, but to actually look at as a shape.

Write out your full fragility profile in one place:

My wealth in months is ______. My income concentration is ______ percent from a single source. My debt composition is mostly flexible / mostly rigid / a mix of both. My estimated re-employability speed is ______ weeks or months. My protection gap is currently open / partially covered / covered.

Look at what you've written. Where does the real weak point actually sit? For a lot of people, it's not the savings number at all, it's the income concentration, or the protection gap, sitting quietly unaddressed while all the attention goes toward the buffer. Knowing exactly where your specific weak point sits is what makes the modules ahead useful instead of generic. You're not about to follow a one-size-fits-all plan. You're about to follow one built on your actual shape.
