Why Job Titles Hide More Than They Show

The Market Does Not Buy Titles.
It Buys Proven Ability.

A job title is a short label written for internal use inside one company. It is almost useless for showing what you actually did, and what problems you are able to solve for someone else.

The first real task of a skills audit is setting the title aside completely, and asking a plainer, more honest question: What did I actually do, day to day, and what result did that produce?

The Curse of Knowledge

The "Second Nature" Trap

There is a specific trap that shows up constantly. Certain skills feel worthless to you simply because they've become second nature. You've done them so many times that you've stopped seeing them as skills.

If a skill feels boring or obvious to you, that is not proof it lacks value. It is very often proof that you've simply had it long enough to stop noticing it. Someone outside your field would happily pay for it.

Run a "Boring" Skill Through the Filter

Think of a task at work that you find completely ordinary, tedious, or routine. Check the boxes that apply to it:

Do people occasionally come to you for help with this?
Would it take a completely new hire at least 3 months to do it as fast as you do?
If you stopped doing it, would something important quietly break or slow down?

You Are Undervaluing This Asset.

You checked multiple boxes. This is not just a "basic task"—it is a piece of operational leverage. Because it doesn't challenge you anymore, your brain has mislabeled it as "low value." To an outside client or a new employer, this is exactly what they pay for: smooth, thoughtless competence.

Turning Raw Material Into Things That Sell

The Consultant Translation

Internal corporate language does not sell well on the outside. Every entry in your audit should follow a simple shape: a strong action, a clear scope, and a real result.

A word of real caution: If you honestly don't have a hard number, do not invent one. Describe the real scale instead (team size, time taken, stakes). A made-up number that falls apart under questioning will cost you far more trust than an honest description ever would.
