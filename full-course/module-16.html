Turning A Big Idea Into One Clear Number

Back in the first module, you learned what financial freedom really means. Your money, without you working, brings in enough to cover your life. That's a strong idea, but on its own, it's still just an idea. You can't walk toward something you haven't measured. This chapter turns that idea into one clear, personal number, so you know exactly what you're building toward, and exactly how far you still have to go.

The Simple Formula

Financial freedom happens the moment this becomes true:

Money your assets pay you each month, without you working, is equal to or more than your monthly cost of living.

That's the whole idea. Not a huge pile of cash sitting somewhere. Just enough steady income, from things you own, to cover your real life, month after month, without you trading your time for it.

Step One: Know Your Real Monthly Number

Go back to the number you worked out in the buffer chapter, your full monthly cost of living. This is the base of everything in this chapter, so make sure it's honest and complete. Rent or mortgage, food, transport, electricity, water, phone, insurance, any regular loan payments, and anything else that comes out every single month without fail.

Don't use a number that assumes a smaller, simpler life than the one you actually want. If your real goal includes a bit more comfort than your bare minimum spending today, add that in now, so your number reflects the actual life you're aiming for, not just survival.

Step Two: Turn That Monthly Number Into A Yearly Number

Multiply your monthly number by twelve. This gives you the total amount your assets would need to produce in a full year to fully cover your life without you working.

Example. If your monthly cost of living is 60,000, your yearly number is 720,000.

Step Three: Work Out The Size Of Assets You'd Need

This is the part that turns your yearly number into something concrete. A simple, widely used way to think about this is the four percent guide. It says that if you can safely draw about four percent of your total invested assets each year, your money can keep paying you for a very long time, because the assets keep growing enough, on average, to replace what you draw out.

To use this guide, take your yearly number and multiply it by twenty-five. This gives you a rough estimate of the total size of assets you'd need for those assets to safely produce your yearly number forever, without running out.

Continuing the example. A yearly number of 720,000, multiplied by twenty-five, gives a target of 18,000,000 in invested assets.

This is not a small number, and it's not meant to feel small. It's meant to be honest. This is your actual freedom number, the real size of the target you're building toward.

Why This Number Doesn't Need To Feel Scary

The first time people see their real freedom number, it can feel far away, even discouraging. That reaction is normal, but it misses something important. This number is not something you're expected to reach next year. It's a target for the entire second half of this course, and for the years of building ahead of you, using every tool you've already learned, earning more, managing well, protecting the downside, and, starting in the next module, building real assets on purpose.

It's also worth remembering something from Module 2. Your freedom number isn't only reached by saving harder. It's reached faster by combining steady saving with real income growth and smart investing, all working together, exactly the tools this course has already been building in you.

A Second, Smaller Number Worth Knowing Too

Alongside your full freedom number, it's worth calculating a smaller, nearer target: partial freedom. This is the point where your assets cover a real, meaningful part of your monthly cost, even if not all of it, enough to lower how hard you need to work, or to make one part of your life feel less tied to your paycheck.

Example. If your assets could produce 30,000 a month, and your full cost of living is 60,000, you've already reached half of your freedom number. That's not the finish line, but it's a real, honest milestone worth noticing and celebrating, instead of only focusing on the far away final number.

Keep This Number Alive, Not Buried

A number you calculate once and never look at again doesn't help you. Revisit your freedom number at least once a year, or any time your life changes in a big way, a new child, a big move, a shift in income. Your number will change as your life changes, and that's normal. What matters is that you always know, clearly, exactly what you're aiming for, instead of chasing a vague feeling of "enough" that never actually arrives.

Step 15: Calculate Your Own Freedom Number

Work through this fully with your real numbers.

1. Write your honest full monthly cost of living, including the comfortable version of your life you're actually aiming for, not just your bare minimum spending today.

2. Multiply that number by twelve. Write your full yearly number.
3. Multiply your yearly number by twenty-five. Write your full freedom number.

1. How does it feel to see that number written down clearly? Write your honest first reaction.

2. Right now, roughly how much do you currently hold in real invested assets, stocks, funds, property, a business, anything actually producing or capable of producing income?

3. Based on that current number, roughly what percentage of your full freedom number have you already reached?

4. Calculate your partial freedom number, the point where your assets could cover half your monthly cost of living. Write that number clearly.

5. Of everything you've learned so far in this course, earning more, managing well, protecting the downside, which one do you think will move you toward this number the fastest from here?

6. What is one honest adjustment you could make in the next few months, in income, spending, or investing, that would meaningfully speed up your path toward this number?

7. Write the date, at least a year from now, when you'll come back and recalculate this number properly.
