The Mix Should Change
As You Change

The right mix of assets shifts as you move through life. A bad year at age 25 is a blip. A bad year at age 58 is a crisis.

The "110 Minus Age" Rule

A simple starting guide: take 110 and subtract your current age. The result is the rough percentage you should hold in higher-growth/higher-risk assets. The rest sits in steady, lower-risk holdings (bonds, cash, fixed deposits) as your "dry powder".

This ensures you are never forced to sell your growth investments at a loss just to cover living costs during a bad year. It’s not retreating from wealth building—it’s protecting it.

Step 20 Integration

Plan Your Own Approach

Question 01Using the 110-minus-your-age guide, calculate your own rough starting mix (Growth vs Steadier).
Question 02Roughly, what does your actual current mix look like right now across all your savings and assets?
Question 03Is your current mix carrying more risk than the guide suggests, less, or roughly in line?
Question 04If it is carrying more risk, what specifically would need to shift, and how?
Question 05How many years do you honestly expect before you'd want to start drawing real income from these assets?
Question 06Does anyone currently depend on your income? Should that make you lean slightly more toward steady assets?
Question 07Of everything you hold, which pieces do the job of growth, and which pieces do steadiness? Is the split clear?
Question 08If markets had a genuinely bad year tomorrow, would you be forced to sell growth assets at a loss to live?
Question 09What is one specific adjustment you could make this year to bring your mix closer to where it should be?
Question 10Write down the date you'll review this mix again, at least a year from now.
