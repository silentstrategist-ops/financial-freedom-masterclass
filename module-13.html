Module 13 · Pressure Testing Defences

A Real Buffer
Makes You A Target.

The exact moment you build real surplus is the moment you become a target. Scams are engineered to defeat normal judgement. You need a mechanical filter.

DIAGNOSTIC ENGINE 01

The 7-Point Filter

Think of a specific financial opportunity someone recently pitched to you. Run it through this diagnostic grid. Treat any single flagged item as a serious warning.

No Verifiable Information
You cannot find independent, verifiable information about this opportunity from a source entirely disconnected to the person pitching it.
Guaranteed or Fixed Returns
The return is promised as specific, unusually high, and "guaranteed". (Remember: if there is zero risk, there is zero justification for a high return).
Manufactured Urgency
You are being pushed to decide quickly ("only three spots left", "closes tonight") to prevent you from taking a week to think slowly.
Recruitment Reliant
Your potential income is tied more to recruiting new people beneath you than to selling a real product to genuine outside customers.
Isolation Tactics
You have been discouraged (even subtly) from discussing this with a spouse, advisor, or someone you trust outside the opportunity.
No Real Track Record
The person pitching this does not have a genuine, verifiable track record in this specific industry, they are simply confident and likeable.
Hostility to Questions
Asking detailed questions makes you "negative" or they say you "just don't understand how this works".

Step 12: Pressure Test Your Own Defences

Question 01Has anyone ever approached you, directly or through your existing relationships, with an opportunity promising guaranteed or unusually high returns? What happened, and did you commit any money?
Question 02Looking back at that situation using the checklist above, how many of the seven items would that opportunity have failed?
Question 03Think of a moment you felt pressured to decide quickly about money. What specific tactic was used, and how did it actually affect your thinking in the moment?
Question 04Is there a current relationship in your life where a recruitment based opportunity has been presented to you? Walk through the core question honestly: is the money coming from real outside sales, or from new recruits joining?
Question 05Who in your life would you genuinely trust to give you an honest, disinterested second opinion before you commit meaningful money to anything? Have you actually used that person recently?
Question 06If someone discouraged you from asking detailed questions about an opportunity, or made you feel foolish for asking, how did you respond at the time, and how would you want to respond now?
Question 07What is the most convincing version of a guaranteed return pitch you've personally encountered? What made it feel credible, specifically?
Question 08Commit to memory, and write out in your own words, the single question from this chapter you'll ask yourself before any future opportunity involving your money.
Question 09If you have, in the past, lost money to something matching the patterns in this chapter, what did you learn from it that you're now able to name clearly?
Question 10Write down the exact seven item checklist from this chapter somewhere you'll actually see it again, and commit to using it before your next financial decision.
