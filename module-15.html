The Events Your Buffer
Was Never Built For.

Your buffer is strong. It can handle a broken appliance, a slow month, a small surprise bill. But there's a whole other category of event that a buffer, even a big one, cannot survive.

Nobody enjoys thinking about the day they get seriously sick, or the day they're no longer around for the people who depend on them. It feels heavy, and it's easy to tell yourself you'll deal with it later, once things settle down, once you have more money, once life feels calmer.

Here's the honest truth worth sitting with: These events don't wait for a calm, convenient time. And the cost of being unprepared, both in money and in stress on the people you love, is far higher than the small, steady cost of being covered ahead of time. This chapter isn't here to scare you. It's here so that if something serious does happen, it becomes a hard day, not a total collapse.

The Main Types of Protection

The 4 Pillars of Real Cover

⚕️
Health Cover

Protects you from the cost of medical care, hospital stays, treatment, medicine. A serious illness or accident without health cover can eat through years of savings in weeks.

I have adequate health cover
🕊️
Life Cover

Pays out a set amount to the people who depend on you if you pass away. If anyone relies on your income, this is not optional. It is a direct plan for what happens to them.

I have adequate life cover
♿
Disability Cover

If an illness or injury stops you from working for a long time, but doesn't take your life, you still need income. This replaces part of your income while you're unable to work.

I have disability cover
🏠
Asset Cover

Protects specific big items (a home, a car, a business) against damage, theft, or loss. If losing that one item would set you back years, it needs its own specific protection.

My major assets are covered
Step 14 Integration

Face Your Own Protection Gap

Question 01Does anyone currently depend on your income, fully or partly? Name who, and what would actually happen to them if your income stopped tomorrow.
Question 02Do you currently have any life cover? If yes, is the amount enough to actually replace your income for the people who depend on you, or was it chosen without much thought?
Question 03Do you currently have any health cover? If yes, do you actually know what it covers and what it leaves out?
Question 04If you were seriously injured or ill and couldn't work for six months, where would the money to live on actually come from?
Question 05Do you have any form of disability cover? If not, was that a decision you made on purpose, or something you simply never got around to?
Question 06Is there one asset in your life, a home, a car, tools you use for work, that would be a major setback to lose? Is it currently protected?
Question 07Of everything covered in this chapter, which single gap feels the most urgent to close first, based on your real situation?
Question 08What has actually been stopping you from closing that gap until now? Cost, time, discomfort thinking about it, or something else?
Question 09What is one small, real step you could take in the next thirty days to move on that first gap, even if it doesn't fully close it yet?
Question 10Write down the date you'll take that step by, and who, if anyone, could help you understand your actual options properly.
